A quiet, cinematic composition. One object only: a small rosewood box with pale shell inlay set into its lid in a few quiet geometric pieces, placed left of centre on a dark wooden surface that runs out of the frame to the right. The box takes a little more than a quarter of the picture; the breathing space is the soft transition from table to a warm grey plaster wall behind it, plus the shallow focus that lets the far wall dissolve, rather than a deliberately blank half. The rosewood is a deep warm purple-brown with fine close grain and a hand-polished sheen; the inlay is cool shell white with a faint pearl edge. Warm side light from a high window grazes the lid, so the inlay catches a thin cool highlight while the box's side falls into deep brown shadow.

The palette is restrained: rosewood purple-brown, shell white, deep shadow brown and one narrow warm highlight. Materials are tactile and specific: tight wood grain, hairline seams around each inlay piece, worn edges, a satin rather than lacquered finish.
