Create an original fine-art image of a Song dynasty palace hall interior, imagined rather than documented, as the source artwork for a curated contemporary colour palette titled "朱漆鎏金" (Vermilion Lacquer, Gilded). This is a poetic reconstruction of imperial Song taste, not a museum visualisation and not a reproduction of a specific building.

The scene is rich, layered and detailed: a deep hall seen from inside, with vermilion-lacquered timber columns, painted bracket sets and architraves in Song mineral polychrome (azurite blue-green, malachite, cinnabar, ochre, with pale halo bands of graded colour), a carved coffered ceiling touched with dull gold, tall lattice doors whose dense geometric tracery repeats into the distance, and silk hangings gathered at the sides. The floor is laid stone with a worn woven floor covering. In the middle distance a low vermilion lacquer table carries a single gilded lacquer chest: a broad dull matte gilt panel across the lid, worn through in places to the red beneath, gilt corner fittings and rim, with a small gilt clasp. The chest is the warm colour event of the hall. Beyond it, the far doors stand open onto a sunlit courtyard, and two silk lanterns hang in the haze.

Composition carries real spatial depth in three layers: a foreground column and gathered silk, softly out of focus at the left edge; the lit table, chest and floor in the middle ground; the open hall door, lanterns and glowing courtyard beyond. A shaft of late sunlight cuts diagonally through the hall, laying a warm band across the stone floor, grazing the gilt lid and catching dust in the air. The ceiling and the upper half of the far wall dissolve into deep, quiet shadow, and one broad panel of the floor is left in plain shadow so the hall still breathes. Density of detail belongs to the architecture; empty air belongs to light and shadow, not to a reserved blank half of the canvas.

Palette: cinnabar and vermilion lacquer, dull antique gold, azurite and malachite blue-green from the painted beams, ochre and ivory from plaster and stone, and warm amber where sunlight hits the floor, all sitting against deep umber shadow.

Art direction: a cinematic film still of an imagined Song imperial interior; painterly, atmospheric realism rather than documentation. Sculpted directional light, gentle bloom around the brightest gilt, subtle air perspective and haze, natural film grain, muted but deep saturation, restrained contrast in the shadows. Rich in detail yet composed, never busy: repetitions of lattice and painted beam do the work, and the palette stays disciplined. Not a tourist photograph, not a flat architectural rendering, not glossy CGI, not a symmetrical postcard of a throne room.

Technical: photorealistic micro-detail at the focal plane, crackled lacquer, worn gilding, flaking mineral paint, dusty stone, woven textile, silk sheen; natural falloff elsewhere. Landscape aspect ratio 3:2, high quality, image fills the entire canvas edge to edge.
